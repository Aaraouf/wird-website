Wird Family vs Ali Huda, Miraj, Noor Kids and AlifBee: what it includes and what it replaces

If you are a parent paying for a kids' Islamic app, you are probably paying between $80 and $90 a year for it, and you are probably using a free prayer app with ads for yourself. A family Islamic app that covers you and your children under one plan did not exist in this category until now. Wird Family, from Wird by wirdwellness.app, is that plan: the parent's own prayer and Qur'an practice, plus Wird Kids on up to five iPads, for $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year.

Last updated 29 October 2026. Wird is coming to the App Store; prices are in US dollars and the App Store shows your local currency.

The question that matters is not whether Family is cheaper than Ali Huda, Miraj Stories, Noor Kids or AlifBee Kids. It is. The question is whether it replaces what you already pay for, or just adds to it. This page answers that row by row, and it names the rows where a kids' library is the better buy.

Wird and Wird Kids are coming to the App Store. Family opens when Wird Kids is listed with its Juz Amma packs, before the first Wird-wide challenge on 18 January 2027.

What is Wird Family?

Wird Family is a household plan for one parent and up to five children aged 5 to 10. The parent gets the full Wird app with every Plus feature; each child gets Wird Kids on an iPad, with prayer stars, short surahs, prophets' stories told without faces and a tree that never dies. It costs $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year with a 14-day free trial, and nothing devotional is behind the price.

What Wird Family replaces, and what it does not

This is the honest centre of the page. Read it before the price. If you want a checklist to run on any kids' app, including this one, it is in Islamic apps for kids aged 5 to 10: what parents should check.

Family replaces a kids' app if what you were buying was a habit. If you paid for Ali Huda or Miraj because you wanted your child to have something Islamic on the iPad every day, and what actually happened was a few videos a week, then Family is the thing you were reaching for. Prayer stars, a short surah to practise, a tree that grows, and a parent who can see it from their own phone and send a cheer.

Family does not replace a library. Ali Huda has a deep video catalogue. Miraj has years of stories and games. Noor Kids sends a physical book to your door every month. Wird Kids has prophets' stories, told without faces and released one at a time after review, and that is a small shelf next to a library. If your child watches an hour of Ali Huda a week and loves it, keep it. Family is not that.

Family does not teach Arabic. AlifBee Kids is an Arabic course, and Wird Kids is not. The surahs in Wird Kids are practised in Arabic, with recite-back checked on the device, but that is memorisation practice, not language teaching. If AlifBee is doing its job, nothing here competes with it.

Family adds the parent. This is the row no kids' app can fill. The $59.99 includes the full adult app with every Plus feature: the Fajr alarm, the full widget set, posting in Together, Ask without the daily cap, insights and memorisation tracking. A parent who would have bought Wird Plus at $39.99 for themselves is paying $20 more a year for up to five children.

Why a household plan did not exist

Kids' Islamic apps are content businesses. A library is expensive to make and cheap to sell again, so it makes sense to sell it per child and keep adding to it. Adult prayer apps are utilities or, like Muslim Pro, ad businesses. Neither model has a reason to look at the other end of the household.

Wird started from the other side: a tree that grows when the parent prays, and a question from parents about whether the child could have one too. Wird Kids was the answer, and the family garden, where a parent sees each child's tree from their own phone, followed. Family is the plan that puts both under one price. The plans page lists every line of it.

Who this is for

The parent already paying $80 to $90 a year for one kids' app, and using a free prayer app with ads. Family costs less than the kids' app alone, covers you as well, and has no ads anywhere. Whether it replaces the kids' app depends on the row above: habit, yes; library, no.

The parent of two or more children. Every kids' app on this page is priced for the child, and whether one price covers siblings was not checked. Family covers up to five iPads for one price, and the siblings' leaderboard is built for exactly this house.

The parent who wants to try before paying a year. Family has a 14-day free trial, and Wird Kids on one iPad is free with no trial at all, so a one-child family can live on it for as long as they like. If a family stops using the paid plan in the first 30 days, ask and the year is refunded.

The parent who wants to know who reviewed the content. Wird's content rule is that it never speaks for the sources: Qur'an text from the mushaf with a named translation, hadith only from Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim by reference, Wird's own words labelled as Wird's own. Each prophet's story is reviewed before it is released, and they are released one at a time for that reason. There is no named scholar on the website yet; until there is, that sentence is the whole claim, and nothing more is promised.

The parent who has heard that kids' apps run out. A library ends when the videos run out. A tree does not. That is an argument, not a measurement, and it is labelled as one.

Objections

"I will not pay for another subscription." Then do not add one. Family makes sense only as a replacement. If you cannot cancel the kids' app, the free Wird Kids iPad and the free adult app are still the whole practice, and they cost nothing.

"My child will not use it unless they ask for it." Probably true, and the free Kids iPad is how they find out. Nobody should buy Family before the child has planted a tree.

"I want to see other parents' reviews first." Wird is pre-launch, so there are none, and this page will not invent any. When the App Store listing is live, its ratings are the place to look, not this page.

"$149.99 once is a lot." It is about two and a half years of Family paid at once, and it is sold only between Ramadan and Eid 2027 for people who refuse renewals. Outside that window the yearly plan is the same thing, paid as you go.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a family plan for an Islamic kids' app?

Wird Family is a household plan: the parent's full Wird app plus Wird Kids on up to five iPads, for $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year with a 14-day trial. Ali Huda, Miraj Stories, Noor Kids and AlifBee Kids are each sold per child account and do not include anything for the parent.

Does Wird Family replace Ali Huda or Miraj Stories?

It replaces them if what you wanted was a daily Islamic habit for your child: prayer stars, short surahs, a tree that grows, stories released one at a time. It does not replace them if what your child uses is the video or game library itself; Wird Kids is not a library and does not try to be one.

Are Islamic kids' apps worth the subscription price?

They are worth it when the child actually uses the library every week. At $79.99 to $124.99 a year, the honest test is to count the hours used last month. Wird Family is $59.99 a year for the parent and up to five children, and Wird Kids on one iPad is free, so a family can find out whether a habit app sticks before paying anything.

What does Wird Kids include?

Prayer stars for each prayer, short surahs with recite-back checked on the iPad, prophets' stories told without faces, a tree that never dies, and a family leaderboard. It is for children aged 5 to 10, on iPad, and the child's screen never shows a price. One iPad is free; Family links up to five to the parent's phone.

Is there an Islamic app for parents and kids together?

Wird is the one with both ends under one plan: the parent's five prayers, Qur'an and habits in Wird, each child's prayer stars and surahs in Wird Kids, and a family garden where the parent sees every child's tree from their own phone. Family challenges, starting with 21 days to Ramadan in January 2027, are joined as a household.

How much does Wird Family cost compared with Ali Huda?

Wird Family is $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year. Ali Huda is listed at $7.99 a month or $79.99 a year with a one-week trial. The $20 difference buys the parent's own app and up to four more children, and loses the video library. Prices are App Store listings as of 2026-10-28.
