Wird vs Muslim Pro and other ad-funded prayer apps

Most people searching for a Muslim Pro alternative are not looking for more features. They have the features. They are looking for a prayer app that does not show an ad at adhan time, does not nudge them toward a subscription every few days, and does not keep a record of them they did not ask for. This page compares Wird by wirdwellness.app with Muslim Pro, and with the two apps people most often move to, Pillars and Athan Pro, on those terms and a few others.

Last updated 29 October 2026. Wird is coming to the App Store; prices are in US dollars and the App Store shows your local currency.

A fair warning before the table. Wird is not a Muslim Pro replacement in every column. Muslim Pro is a prayer times, Qur'an and adhan app with a very large user base and a content library. Wird is a daily prayer and Qur'an habit app with prayer times as a utility inside it. Several rows below go to Muslim Pro or Pillars, and the page says so, because a comparison that only its author could win is not worth your time.

Wird is coming to the App Store, so this page compares what Wird launches with, not a track record.

What is the difference between Wird and Muslim Pro?

Muslim Pro is an ad-funded prayer times and Qur'an app with paid tiers that remove the ads and add content. Wird is a prayer and Qur'an habit app with no ads on any plan, where all worship is free and paid plans add only the layer around it. If you want prayer times and a big library, Muslim Pro or Pillars; if you want a calm daily practice that never gates worship, Wird.

How this comparison was made

Every price on this page is as listed on the US or Canadian App Store on 2026-10-28, linked to the listing or the only public source available. Prices vary by region and change often; treat each as a snapshot. Where the only source for a number is a third-party tracker or a rival's blog, the row says so.

The criteria are the ones people actually raise when they leave an app, taken from public reviews rather than from a feature list:

  1. Ads, especially at adhan time or in the Qur'an.
  2. What is free, and what is behind a price. Specifically, whether anything devotional is gated.
  3. Price and billing, including whether a one-time purchase exists.
  4. Prayer times and adhan, which is the job most people install a prayer app for.
  5. Data and privacy, including whether an account is required and where location goes.
  6. What it does with a missed prayer, because the apps that track prayer differ most here.

Wird, in its own words

Wird is a calm daily companion for the five prayers, the Qur'an and the small habits around them. Every prayer you log waters a drawn tree that grows through nine stages over months. On quiet days the leaves dry a little; the trunk stands, one bud always survives, and when you come back the leaves lift again. Nothing in it is a score.

Wird stands out from Muslim Pro and Athan Pro because there are no ads on any plan, and from every app in the category because worship is free in writing: the five prayers, the tree, the whole Qur'an reader, dhikr and your history cost nothing, ever. What you pay for is only the layer around it. The full list is on the plans page.

Wird is weaker than Muslim Pro and Pillars on prayer times as a product. It has prayer times and reminders, and they work, but it does not aim to be the best adhan app on your phone. The honest advice, from Wird itself, is to keep your adhan app if you love it and let Wird be the tree next to it. There is a longer page on that distinction.

Muslim Pro

Muslim Pro is the default Muslim app. It has prayer times, adhan, a Qur'an reader, and a content library under Qalbox. Its owner reported 25 million monthly users in 2024, according to the New Straits Times. It is listed at $3.99 a month, about $29.99 to $34.99 a year, with higher $12.99 a month and $49.99 a year tiers also listed on the App Store.

What people leave it over is consistent across sources. A one-star Trustpilot review over ads and subscription pressure ends with "stay away from this app" (Trustpilot). A Play Store reviewer wrote that they paid for lifetime and still saw ads (justuseapp). And the company keeps a support article titled Is it acceptable to pay for a religious application?, which is as clear a statement of the objection as any reviewer's.

Rows Muslim Pro wins: prayer times depth, the size of its library, and the plain fact that the people around you already have it, which matters for sharing. If you want one app for everything and do not mind ads or a subscription, it is the obvious choice.

Pillars

Pillars is the app people most often name when they want prayer times without ads. It is free, calm, and well reviewed, and what it sells is cosmetic: themes, Apple Watch support, supporter birds and alternative icons, under a supporter subscription that a third-party page lists "from $4.99/mo" (unverified; the App Store listing is the place to check).

Pillars wins the "free prayer times, no ads" row outright, and Wird does not try to take it. If you are leaving Muslim Pro only because of ads, Pillars is probably your answer and you can stop reading here. Where Wird adds something is after the adhan: logging the prayer, keeping a Qur'an habit, the nightly check-in, and a tree that records your return rather than your lapse. Many people will run both.

Athan Pro

Athan Pro is a prayer times and adhan app at the low end of the market, about $2.99 a month with a lifetime option around $19.99 according to aggregator figures (the listing is the source of truth). Its free tier is ad-funded, and a published review summary describes ads "loading at the moment users open the app to check or hear the adhan" (unstar).

Athan Pro wins on price for a one-time purchase if the aggregator figure holds. It loses on the one thing most people in this search are leaving over, which is an ad in front of the adhan.

Verdict: which should you use?

If you want prayer times and nothing else, with no ads: Pillars. Free, no ads, established. Wird is not a better adhan app than Pillars and does not claim to be.

If you want one app that does everything and you accept ads or a subscription: Muslim Pro. Its depth and its library are real, and 25 million monthly users is a number no challenger can argue with.

If you keep falling off prayer and coming back, and you want an app that treats coming back as the whole practice: Wird. No ads on any plan, worship free in writing, a missed prayer that reads "Make up" instead of red, and a tree that never dies. Keep Pillars or Muslim Pro for the adhan if you like.

If you are a parent of a child aged 5 to 10: Wird is the only app on this page with a children's companion and a household plan. The Family comparison is the page for that decision.

If you refuse subscriptions: Wird Plus can be bought once for $49.99, and Athan Pro lists a lifetime around $19.99. Muslim Pro's listing shows no one-time option.

Who this is for

The person leaving Muslim Pro over ads. You are not wrong that an ad at adhan time changes what the moment is. Pillars solves the adhan; Wird solves what comes after it. You lose Muslim Pro's library.

The person who tried a prayer tracker and quit when the streak broke. Muslim Pro, Pillars and Athan Pro are not trackers, so they never hurt you this way, and they never helped either. Wird's answer to the streak is the tree; the full argument is here.

The person who wants to pay once and be done. Wird Plus at $49.99 once, Athan Pro's lifetime if the figure holds. Neither Muslim Pro nor Pillars lists a one-time purchase.

The person who does not want to be tracked by a worship app. Wird needs no account, keeps precise location on the phone, and uses no third-party analytics or tracking SDKs. The plain-English version is in what Wird can and cannot see. Muslim Pro's and Pillars' policies are theirs to read.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an alternative to Muslim Pro without ads or a subscription?

Pillars is free with no ads and sells only cosmetic extras, so it is the closest like-for-like alternative for prayer times. Wird has no ads on any plan and keeps all worship free; its paid plans add the layer around worship, and Plus can be bought once for $49.99 instead of subscribing. Which you choose depends on whether you want an adhan app or a daily practice app.

Wird vs Muslim Pro: which is better?

Neither, in general. Muslim Pro is better for prayer times depth, a large content library and the fact that most people already have it. Wird is better if you want no ads on any plan, worship that is free in writing, and a tree instead of a streak for keeping prayer through good and bad weeks. Many people would keep Muslim Pro or Pillars for the adhan and use Wird for the practice.

Does Wird have prayer times and adhan?

Yes, with reminders, free on every plan. They are a utility inside Wird rather than its main product, so if you already love your adhan app, keep it. Wird is the tree next to it, not a replacement for it.

How much does Muslim Pro cost compared with Wird?

Muslim Pro is listed at $3.99 a month and about $29.99 to $34.99 a year, with higher $12.99 a month and $49.99 a year tiers also listed. Wird's worship is free; Wird Plus is $4.99 a month, $39.99 a year or $49.99 once, and Wird Family is $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year. Prices are US and Canadian App Store listings as of 2026-10-28 and change over time.

Muslim Pro vs Pillars vs Wird: which prayer app should I use?

Pillars if you want free prayer times with no ads. Muslim Pro if you want everything in one app and accept ads or a subscription. Wird if you want a daily prayer and Qur'an habit with no ads, nothing devotional behind a price, and a tree that never resets you to zero. They are not exclusive; Pillars plus Wird is a common pairing.

Does Wird sell my data or location?

No. Wird needs no account, precise location never leaves the phone, and there are no ads or third-party analytics or tracking SDKs in the app. There are no public counts of anything devotional, and you can delete everything from inside the app.

Where this leaves you

Before: a prayer app that shows an ad when the adhan plays and asks every few days whether you have upgraded yet. After: an adhan app you trust for the times, and next to it a tree that grows when you pray and waits when you cannot, with nothing devotional behind a price.

One number to hold onto: Muslim Pro has 25 million monthly users, and the single most repeated reason people give for leaving is an ad in front of worship. That is the whole gap Wird is built in.
